Tesla Wolfe is a professional dancer, choreographer, actress, model, producer, and entrepreneur from Ada, Oklahoma, of Seminole, Creek, and Absentee Shawnee descent — the first Native American model ever featured in Maxim Magazine. A former Vegas showgirl at the Bellagio's Mayfair Supper Club and choreographer for Insomniac Entertainment's EDC Las Vegas Mainstage, her screen credits include Yellowstone and Interior Chinatown. She is signed with Wilhelmina Modeling Agency and NTA Talent Agency.

She trained at the Lee Strasberg Film & Theatre Institute and the Ivana Chubbuck School of Acting, and attended the University of Central Oklahoma as a Dance major with a minor in Psychology. Her movement background spans ballet, jazz, contemporary, hip-hop, and pole, sharpened over years as a company member with 8&1 Professional Jazz & Contemporary Company.

Beyond performance, Tesla is the Founder and Artistic Director of Electric Turquoise Entertainment — a 501(c)(3) nonprofit she has personally funded from the ground up to open doors for Native, Indigenous, and underprivileged performing artists. Her reels, full acting and dance credits, and her nonprofit's story follow below.

Founder & Artistic Director

Electric Turquoise

501(c)(3) Native American Performing Arts Nonprofit

"It's Our Time So Let The Rez Kids Shine."

Electric Turquoise began the way most movements do — with one person deciding the wait was over. Tesla Wolfe founded the organization to give Native and Indigenous performing artists the training, mentorship, and stage that Indian Country had never fully been offered on its own terms.

Rather than wait on outside funding to prove the vision, Tesla built Electric Turquoise from the ground up herself — personally investing more than $90,000 of her own earnings from her modeling, acting, and dance career directly into workshops, productions, costuming, and scholarships before ever asking for a dollar of outside support. That self-funded groundwork built the organization's first stages, first cohort of dancers, and first proof that the vision works.

Mission

To inspire and empower Native American communities through Indigenous performing arts, language preservation, and cultural wellness — turning trauma and limited resources into creative power, one artist at a time.
